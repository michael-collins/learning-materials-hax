Models often end up with problems you can't see at first: faces turned inside out, faces that overlap or are missing, and vertices that have drifted out of line. This troubleshooting part goes through fixing them on an Airline Chair model: flipping normals, cleaning up and rebuilding the end of the arm, and lining up its vertices with scaling and snapping. Use it when your own chair from Parts 1 to 6 shades strangely or smooths badly. The video uses Blender 2.90.

Fix faces that point the wrong way

  1. Turn on Face Orientation. 0:00 Open the Viewport Overlays menu and tick Face Orientation. It colors each face by which way its normal points: blue is good, red is the wrong way (inside out).

  2. Flip the normals. 0:21 Select the red object, press Tab and select all its faces (A). Choose Mesh → Normals → Flip: the faces turn blue. Do the same for any other red pieces 0:35.

Find the problem

  1. Look at it on its own, in wireframe. 0:45 Whenever you see geometry out of alignment, take the time to fix it. Turn on the wireframe overlay to see what's going on, and press Shift+H to hide everything except the selected piece 0:58. Turn off its Mirror modifier for now. Here, the end of the arm has edges far out of line 1:05.

  2. Frame what you're fixing. 1:30 To focus on one part, select its vertices and choose View → Frame Selected. If you'll do it often, right-click it and add it to your Quick Favorites: then press Q and choose Frame Selected 1:45.

  3. Spot non-manifold geometry. 1:55 Here an edge is missing, so this piece is non-manifold: its surface isn't closed, and couldn't exist as a real object. There's more odd geometry further back 2:11. The simplest fix is to remove it and rebuild.

Remove the bad faces

  1. Delete the faces. 2:15 In Edit Mode, press 3 for face select and select the faces that are wrong. Press X for the Delete menu and choose Faces. Repeat until only good geometry is left 2:30.

Rebuild the surface

  1. Fill a face between edges. 3:00 That leaves a hole to fill. Select a couple of edges on either side of a gap and press F to make a face between them.

  2. Add the missing edges with the Knife. 3:20 To add edges where they're missing, use the Knife tool (K) in vertex mode: click one vertex, then the next, and press Enter 3:35. It doesn't always show a preview, so trust that it snaps to the vertex you click. If an edge doesn't come out, check in face mode that there's no stray face in the gap, and delete it if there is 4:00.

  3. Fill the rest. 4:20 Select the edges around the remaining hole and press F again. Alt+click selects a whole edge loop, and Shift+Alt+click adds one to the selection 4:29.

Line up the vertices

  1. Flatten a row of vertices. 4:35 Go to vertex select, turn on X-ray and go to the side view. Select the row of vertices along the bottom and scale them along Z (S, Z) until they're in a straight line 4:52. Don't cross the origin as you scale: a negative scale turns the row over.

  2. Snap vertices onto others. 4:57 To slide vertices across to match the others, turn on snapping and choose Vertex as what it snaps to 5:10. With the Transform tool, drag along one axis and hover over the vertex you want to match: the movement stays on that axis and lines up with it 5:18.

Rebuild the drop at the end

  1. Extrude the faces down. 5:25 In face mode, select the faces along the bottom of the arm's end and press E to extrude them straight down. The reference image isn't linked in this file (it shows as solid pink), so the depth is a guess 5:40. Press Alt+H to show everything again and compare: here it needed to go a bit lower, so extrude again along Z 6:05. Snapping is still on, so turn it off first 6:20.

  2. Tidy the corner. 6:35 Finally, select the vertices at the corner that have moved and put them back in place.

Wrap-up

Face Orientation shows inside-out faces, which Mesh → Normals → Flip fixes. For stray, overlapping or missing faces, isolate the piece, delete what's wrong, and rebuild it with F and the Knife tool. Line up vertices by scaling along one axis or snapping to other vertices, then turn snapping off again.
