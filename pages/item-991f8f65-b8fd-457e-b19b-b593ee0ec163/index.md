Before setting up cameras and scenes of your own, take a tour of the finished animatic's Blender file. You'll see how its scene is built to stay quick to work with, how its animation is only blocked in, and how its cameras and scenes are named to match the renders they make. You won't change anything here; it's a model for organizing your own layout. You should know how to get around Blender's viewport and select objects.

Build the set at real-world scale
---------------------------------

1.  **Look through the camera.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="0">0:00</page-anchor> Press Numpad 0 to go in and out of the active camera's view. The scene is enormous compared with what the camera sees.
    
2.  **Model at real-world size.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="27">0:27</page-anchor> The house is about the size of a real house, in meters, so the lighting can be fairly accurate and the camera's field of view feels natural. Select an object and press N to see its _Dimensions_ in the sidebar: the house is 8.32 m tall, about 30 feet, perhaps a bit big <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="48">0:48</page-anchor>.
    
    ![The house selected in the viewport, with the dock, canoe and a camera in front of it and pines behind. The sidebar's Transform panel shows scale 3 on each axis and Dimensions of 15.5 m, 6 m and 8.32 m.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0052.8.jpg)
    
    The house's dimensions in the sidebar (N): 8.32 m tall.
    

Keep the scene light
--------------------

1.  **Hide what you don't need to see.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="73">1:13</page-anchor> Lots of objects, even copies of the same one, use a lot of memory, and a computer without much spare RAM will slow down. Turn off an object's or collection's viewport visibility but leave its render visibility on: it disappears while you work and still appears when you render <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="101">1:41</page-anchor>. If the Outliner doesn't show those toggles, turn them on from its _Filter_ menu, under _Restriction Toggles_ <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="123">2:03</page-anchor>. The toggle there that stops objects being selected by accident is optional.
    
    ![The Outliner's Filter popover open, with the Restriction Toggles row of icons at the top and a tooltip on one reading Disable in Renders, Globally disable in renders. The viewport behind shows the house and the pines.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0130.0.jpg)
    
    The Outliner's _Filter_ menu, where the viewport and render toggles are turned on.
    
2.  **Make trees with Duplicate Linked.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="139">2:19</page-anchor> Instead of duplicating a tree with Shift+D, which makes a new object from the old one, use _Duplicate Linked_, Alt+D (Option+D on a Mac). It makes an instance of the same object, which you can still scale and rotate on its own, so the trees look different while sharing one mesh.
    
    ![Looking down on the forest from above the house: one tall, thin pine, tree.135, is selected, and the sidebar shows its rotation of minus 63.7 degrees on Z and a scale of 0.101, 0.101 and 1.712.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0155.4.jpg)
    
    One of the instanced trees, scaled and rotated on its own.
    
3.  **Give simple models character with a modifier.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="181">3:01</page-anchor> The house has barely any modeling. A _Displace_ modifier wiggles its vertices up and down (direction _Z_) so it looks more cartoony and haphazardly built. Press Tab to see the mesh without the displacement <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="200">3:20</page-anchor>.
    
    ![The house and dock selected, with the Modifier Properties showing a Displace modifier using Texture.001, Direction Z, Space Local, Strength 0.100 and Midlevel 0.500.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0195.8.jpg)
    
    The house's _Displace_ modifier, set to Z.
    
4.  **Leave expensive things unanimated.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="212">3:32</page-anchor> The water is a plane with an _Ocean_ modifier at a high resolution, making subtle waves and ripples. Animating the waves is very processor intensive, so they're left still; the plane itself was later keyframed to move just up and down <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="227">3:47</page-anchor>.
    
    ![The water selected, a huge gray plane with a scale of 170.364, and the Modifier Properties showing an Ocean modifier: Geometry Generate, resolution 26, Time 1.00, Depth 4 m, Spatial Size 12, and wave Scale 0.010 with Choppiness 1.03. Keyframes for the water show in the timeline.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0215.0.jpg)
    
    The _Ocean_ modifier on the water.
    
5.  **Fake the background with cards.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="245">4:05</page-anchor> The mountains are planes with a few subdivisions and some vertices moved up and down. They're cards in the background, not modeled mountains, and in a low-poly scene that's enough.
    
6.  **Light the sky with an emissive moon.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="286">4:46</page-anchor> The moon's material uses the _Emission_ color of the _Principled BSDF_; an _Emission_ shader would work too. Light from emissive materials doesn't bounce around much in EEVEE anyway: it just needs to be bright. The moon is a circle with its face filled in, one n-gon <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="327">5:27</page-anchor>.
    
    ![The camera view in Material Preview: the moon is selected, glowing pale blue over the dark lake, dock, canoe and pines. Its material settings show Emission set to a light cyan with Emission Strength 1.000.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0304.4.jpg)
    
    The moon's _Emission_ color in the _Principled BSDF_.
    
7.  **Shape the ground with proportional editing.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="343">5:43</page-anchor> The ground is a giant plane with some subdivisions, shaped by moving vertices with proportional editing on. Press G, then Z, and scroll the mouse wheel to change the size of the area it affects <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="362">6:02</page-anchor>. Remember to turn proportional editing off afterwards, or your next edits will behave strangely <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="374">6:14</page-anchor>.
    
    ![The ground plane in Edit Mode, a large grid of vertices, with a vertex being moved along Z with proportional editing. The header reads along global Z (Smooth), Proportional size 66.26.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0365.4.jpg)
    
    Moving the ground's vertices with proportional editing.
    

Block in the animation
----------------------

1.  **Block the action at the right speed.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="378">6:18</page-anchor> The story is about a lonely alien who wants a pet, so it comes down to Earth and finds one. The dog is blocked in: a stand-in for a fully rigged, running dog, moving about as fast as it eventually would and stopping at the end of the dock <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="402">6:42</page-anchor>. The spaceship starts out of the camera's view and is animated coming down.
    
2.  **Keyframe an object's visibility.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="424">7:04</page-anchor> The beam, a cone, appears and disappears because its visibility is animated. Right-click the visibility toggle in the Outliner and choose _Insert Keyframe_. Animate the viewport and render visibility together so you don't get confused <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="439">7:19</page-anchor>. Fading its material's alpha would also work; this is the brute-force way <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="464">7:44</page-anchor>.
    
    ![The beam, a tall cone under the spaceship, selected at frame 76. A context menu from the Outliner is open with Insert Keyframe highlighted and its tooltip, Insert a keyframe for current UI-active property. The Dope Sheet shows the beam's Disable in Viewports and Disable in Renders channels with keyframes.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0440.4.jpg)
    
    Right-click a visibility toggle and choose _Insert Keyframe_.
    
3.  **Go to the frame first, then change and key it.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="488">8:08</page-anchor> Keyframe the off state, move to another frame, and keyframe the on state: the object goes from invisible to visible. A green background shows the toggle is animated <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="497">8:17</page-anchor>; if you change it without inserting a keyframe, you.ve made a change that isn't keyframed yet. So move to the frame you want, change the toggle, then insert the keyframe <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="510">8:30</page-anchor>.
    
4.  **Keep it rough.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="529">8:49</page-anchor> Something fancier is possible, but this is an animatic: it gives the idea of the animation so you can decide how to improve it.
    

Name the cameras and scenes
---------------------------

1.  **Play through the camera.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="550">9:10</page-anchor> From the camera, the dog runs to the end of the dock, then something off frame beams it up. If you already know how you'll cut a shot, you needn't render frames you won't use. If you don't, render the whole thing and decide in the edit <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="582">9:42</page-anchor>.
    
    ![Camera Perspective through cam-1-medium at frame 33: the dog, a low-poly model, runs along the dock beside the red canoe, with pines and the moon behind.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0575.0.jpg)
    
    Frame 33 through _cam-1-medium_.
    
2.  **Name each camera by its shot.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="598">9:58</page-anchor> _cam-1-medium_ is a medium shot; "medium" is just a note to yourself of what kind of shot it is. All the cameras are in their own collection, _cameras_, to keep the Outliner organized <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="622">10:22</page-anchor>.
    
3.  **Switch cameras from the Outliner.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="632">10:32</page-anchor> Click a camera's green camera-data icon in the Outliner to make it the active camera. _cam-2-wide_ shows the mountains, the trees, the whole house, some water and ground: an establishing shot, with the spacecraft coming down into view <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="649">10:49</page-anchor>.
    
    ![Camera Perspective through cam-2-wide, selected in the Outliner's cameras collection: a wide view of the pine forest, the blue house, dock and canoe, and mountains behind.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0698.4.jpg)
    
    The establishing shot through _cam-2-wide_.
    
4.  **Add secondary animation.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="674">11:14</page-anchor> The trees move to show the force of the spaceship as it leaves, and the boat is animated slightly too <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="701">11:41</page-anchor>.
    
5.  **Use scenes for each render.** <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="721">12:01</page-anchor> Blender can keep several scenes in one file, each saving its own settings, such as which camera it uses. The scenes here are named the same way as the render folders, so you can quickly re-render a shot later without working out how it was made <page-anchor target="#video-2eUqeM6cpFY" value="725">12:05</page-anchor>.
    
    ![The Scene selector at the top right of Blender's window showing CAM-2_1-144, with the camera view through cam-2-wide below.](files/a-tour-of-the-layout-scene-t0735.0.jpg)
    
    A scene named like its render folder, _CAM-2\_1-144_.
